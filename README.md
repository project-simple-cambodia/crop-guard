# CropGuard

**Pest or protector? Find out in VR.**

CropGuard is a collaborative virtual reality (VR) learning experience for Cambodian secondary school students. Students explore a 3D rice field, identify pests and their natural enemies, and run experiments that are impossible in a normal classroom.

CropGuard is a Virtual Universe developed by the CADT VR Team within the EU co-funded SIMPLE project.

## The problem

Rice is central to Cambodia: rice paddy covers about 90% of cultivated land, and rice alone provides almost two-thirds of daily calorie intake (Kean, 2012).

- **In the field:** Farmers rank insect pests among the top causes of rice yield loss (Dunn et al., 2023). 99% of farmers rely on chemical insecticides, and 63% mix them (Dunn et al., 2023). Insecticides significantly reduce natural enemy populations (Ngin et al., 2017), and 77% of farmers mistake the lady beetle for a pest; researchers call for training on beneficial insects (Dunn et al., 2023).
- **In the classroom:** Grade 8 and Grade 10 lessons on rice ecosystems are theory-based. Students memorise names from small pictures and never see damage happen. Field trips are costly and seasonal, and real crop damage takes weeks to appear.

## The three Virtual Universes

| Universe | Focus | What students do |
|----------|-------|------------------|
| **VU 1 – Explore & Identify** | Recognition | Identify the rice growth stage (vegetative, reproductive, ripening), then find and classify 23 organisms: 6 pests, 11 predators, 3 parasitoids and 3 neutral species. |
| **VU 2 – Pests & Damage** | Cause and effect | Place an animal on a rice plant. If it is a pest (brown planthopper, yellow stem borer, leaf folder, golden apple snail), the plant shows its real damage stage. If not, the plant stays healthy. |
| **VU 3 – Natural Enemies** | Biological control | Choose from 10 predators and 2 parasitoids. Match the right natural enemy to the target pest and watch the damaged plant recover. |

## How a session works

One headset, a whole team learning. Each team has three roles:

1. **VR Player** – explores the virtual rice field, completes tasks and reports findings.
2. **Manual Reader** – finds information in the reference manual that matches what the player sees.
3. **Worksheet Writer** – records the team's observations and answers.

Sessions run on the **M2L2 (Mobile Metaverse Learning Lab)**: Meta Quest 3 headsets and a local server packed for the classroom. No internet connection is needed, and teachers without a technical background can run a session.

## Key facts

- Platform: Meta Quest 3 (12 headsets in the lab)
- Runs fully offline
- Target learners: Grade 8 and Grade 10
- 23 rice field organisms modelled in 3D
- Team-based learning

## Expected impact

- **Student engagement** – hands-on practice improves engagement and performance.
- **Teacher support** – makes theory that is hard to show visible to students.
- **VR in education** – paves the way for VR in Cambodia's education sector.
- **Sustainable farming** – raises awareness of ecological pest control over pesticide overuse.

## About SIMPLE

SIMPLE (Sustainability Issues Multiverse for building Participatory Learning Environments) is co-funded by the European Union. It explores how virtual reality can help young people understand environmental challenges. In Cambodia, CADT leads the Virtual Universe on the impacts of pesticides on insect biodiversity and sustainable agriculture.

More information: <https://project-simple.eu/>

### Co-funded by

- **European Union** – co-funds the SIMPLE project, helping bring immersive, science-based learning tools to classrooms in Southeast Asia.
- **Global Gateway** – the EU's strategy for investing in sustainable connections worldwide; SIMPLE is part of its effort to strengthen digital education and environmental awareness in partner countries.

### Partners

- **IRD** (Institut de Recherche pour le Développement) – French public research institute; drives SIMPLE's scientific and technical approach, including the GAMA modelling platform.
- **CADT** (Cambodia Academy of Digital Technology) – designs and develops CropGuard and tests it with local students and teachers.
- **CIRAD** – French agricultural research and cooperation organization; brings rice ecosystem and agroecology expertise so the game stays scientifically accurate.
